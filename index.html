    Shafts worth trying in this spine

      Examples only, drawn from long-running product lines – ranges shift between model years, so check the maker's current chart before you buy a dozen.

      How this works: the calculator starts from published target/hunting shaft-selection charts for recurve, compound and traditional bows, then applies the documented adjustments for draw length vs. 28″ limb ratings, point weight, limb material, release type and compound bow speed.

      Recurve and barebow carry a real-world correction. Two independently reported, working setups – a 38lb recurve (W&W NS-G limbs, 100gr point, 28″ draw, 29.5″ arrow) shooting 560-spine A/C/Es, and a 34lb barebow (Uukha limbs, otherwise identical) shooting 620-spine A/C/Es – both tune noticeably weaker than the sourced chart predicts, which matches a common complaint about these charts running stiff. This tool builds that correction into both spine numbers, with barebow getting a further step on top for its plunger-less setup. It has not been re-verified against a wider spread of bow weights, so treat it as a calibrated estimate rather than a manufacturer-verified figure, and it'll only get more accurate as more real setups confirm or shift it. Compound is not corrected this way – it's read straight off the sourced chart, since going too weak there is the direction that can cause a failure at full draw.

      Longbow/traditional works differently. Wood shafts aren't sold by carbon-style spine number – they're graded and sold by the bow weight they suit, so this matches your actual holding weight (adjusted for point weight) directly to that rating, rather than running it through the carbon chart at all. Full carbon isn't offered here for longbow, in keeping with how the discipline is traditionally shot.

      This is a starting point, not a fitting. Confirm any recommendation with paper tuning, bare-shaft testing, or your club's coach before buying arrows – especially on compound bows, where a shaft that's too weak can fail at full draw.
